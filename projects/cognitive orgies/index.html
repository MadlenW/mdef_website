AURA

Cognitive Orgies — Microchallenge I
IAAC / MDEF — 2025

AURA began as an attempt to build an intelligent, agentic system capable of interpreting biometric data and translating it into atmosphere. The idea was to move beyond display and toward mediation — to build a system that would read the electrical noise of a pulse sensor, process it through an AI layer, and decide how a space should respond.

The full technical documentation of the prototype can be found on Hackster.io, but what interests me here is less the outcome and more the traces left by the process.

My initial assumption was that interpretation creates meaning. Raw BPM data felt insufficient. I designed an MCP architecture where an AI agent would categorize emotional states — calm, anxious, excited — and translate those states into environmental outputs. Mediation felt conceptually sophisticated.

The first cognitive rupture came through infrastructure. The network was unstable. Cloudflare tunnels collapsed. Authentication failed. Agentic AI systems revealed themselves as deeply dependent on stable connectivity. Intelligence, I realized, is infrastructural.

A second shift emerged from the pulse itself. Biological rhythms require continuous high-frequency sampling. AI agents operate in query-time. The temporalities did not align. I was trying to interpret life through fragmented requests.

By the third morning, we removed the AI layer entirely. That moment carried a moral trace. The agent architecture had been my contribution. Letting it go meant choosing coherence over ownership.

The system became radically simple: pulse sensor → Pico → LED strip. Direct translation. No categorization. No cloud.

And unexpectedly, that simplicity felt more aligned with my research. The LED pulses in exact rhythm with your heartbeat. It doesn’t interpret you. It doesn’t optimize you. It mirrors you.

Technically, the process required tuning: adaptive baselines, hysteresis-based contact detection, inter-beat smoothing. The body resists clean data. Noise is inherent.

Originally, we aimed to translate pulse into electromagnetic fields. The fields proved too weak. We pivoted to light. Magnetic fields are infrastructural and invisible; light is atmospheric and relational. The pivot reshaped the affective quality of the project.

What remains is a physical question: what if your home knew your heartbeat — not to judge, not to optimize, but simply to glow with you?
