Learning Tools as World-Building

Course documentation & reflection

This course introduced a foundational set of fabrication workflows: laser cutting, CNC milling, 3D printing, silicone mold making and biomaterial casting. Rather than treating them as isolated techniques, I approached them as a connected pipeline—from digital design to material behaviour.

What interests me most is how these tools shift design thinking. They enable modularity, repetition, system logic and material storytelling. Especially through molds and biomaterials, the work moves from producing objects toward designing conditions, processes and variations.

Laser Cutting

Speed, precision and surface logic.

Laser cutting allowed for fast iteration and immediate feedback between drawing and object. Working with perforations, engravings and joints highlighted how small geometric decisions shape both assembly logic and visual language.

CNC Milling

Subtractive fabrication and commitment to form.

CNC milling introduced a slower, more deliberate mode of making. Designing toolpaths required anticipating material resistance, tool diameter and failure before fabrication began. Unlike laser cutting, errors are costly and irreversible, which shifted my attention toward planning, sequencing and structural thinking.

3D Printing

Designing the negative.

3D printing reframed design as preparation for molding. Draft angles, wall thickness and release logic became more important than surface detail. The printed master functions as an intermediary object—its purpose is not use, but replication.

Silicone Mold Making

Replication as a design strategy.

Silicone molding introduced seriality. Once the mold works, the object shifts from singular to repeatable. This process also exposed the importance of timing, mixing ratios, and surface control—precision becomes procedural rather than geometric.

Biomaterials

Material behaviour over control.

Casting biomaterials challenged expectations of consistency. Variations in texture, translucency and brittleness emerged from small changes in mix and inclusions. Instead of eliminating this variability, I see it as a carrier of meaning: each piece records its own conditions of making.

Technical Drawing

Diagramming fabrication logic.

The technical drawing translates the system into parts, layers and relationships. It functions both as instruction and narrative—mapping how components align, interlock and become reproducible.

Relation to My Practice

From tools to systems.

These techniques directly inform my broader work around modular systems, speculative infrastructures and material narratives. Laser cutting and CNC enable structural language, 3D printing and molding support repetition, and biomaterials introduce temporality, decay and ecological agency.
