Reading Machines From The Inside Out

Week 1 reflections

This week we shifted our attention from using technology to reading it as a system. Together with my group, we disassembled a familiar device and traced its internal logic through three shared documents: the Forensic Report, the Component Data Sheet, and the Inventory.

Contributing to the forensic report made me slow down: instead of naming parts, I had to describe behaviours, relations and failure points. This shifted my perspective from “device as object” to “device as layered infrastructure.”

Performing The Machine Paradox

Week 2 reflections — Meluza

This week we shifted from analysing existing devices to building one — not to improve functionality, but to challenge the logic that demands it. Our project Meluza emerged as a response to the machine paradox: the expectation that machines should be purposeful and efficient versus our desire to disrupt that expectation.

Instead of fulfilling user intention, Meluza misbehaves. It invites interaction but resists predictability, creating small moments of confusion or humour. This useless machine exposes how quickly we assume that machines should behave rationally — revealing the fragility of technological trust.

Our short presentation and video documentation capture how Meluza stages this playful but critical tension between expectation and resistance.
